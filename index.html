What counts as a sale or a valid price?

Each row is one completed sale. Total sales includes every row, even with a missing closing date. Price medians use finite prices of at least $1,000; prices above $5 million are retained and disclosed. No missing prices are filled in and no upper cap is applied. City groups use both city and state. These are sample comparisons, not estimates of an entire housing market.

How do we calculate days on market?

Contract date minus list date, in calendar days. Both dates must be valid and the interval nonnegative. Same-day contracts count as zero; missing dates never do. We do not use closing date to measure days on market. Missing listing histories are not random, so timing describes only the usable subset and may underrepresent new construction or off-market sales.

How do we handle duplicates and unreliable fields?

Listing IDs are checked for uniqueness. Repeated street addresses are kept: they can represent separate apartments or separate sales. ZIP codes, county FIPS codes, and parcel numbers remain text. We do not analyze home type because it is unreliable. We also avoid price per square foot, lot size, bathroom, tax, and assessment analyses because of missing or implausible values; assessed value is not sale price.

Where did the data come from?

College-town home sales dataset, version 1.0 (2026). University of Illinois-Deloitte Foundation Center for Business Emerging Technologies. Collected March 2026 from public property listings. Values reflect the collection source and were not verified against county records. Campus assignments use nearest campus coordinates; distance is straight-line, not driving distance. All five supplied dataset files are preserved unchanged.
